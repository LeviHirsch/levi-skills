# Research Report Template

Use this structure when performing a research pass for a learning project. The report should be useful both as a planning input (informing phase design) and as a standalone reference document the learner can revisit.

---

```markdown
# [Topic]: Research Report for [Learning Project Name]

**Purpose:** Inform the learning plan by surveying the domain landscape, existing resources, prior art, and known pitfalls.

---

## Domain Landscape

[Overview of the field/topic. What are the major sub-areas? What's the current state of practice? What are the key debates or schools of thought? This section orients the learner — after reading it, they should understand what they're getting into.]

---

## Existing Curricula & Learning Paths

[What do established courses, bootcamps, or textbooks recommend as a learning order? What progression do experts suggest? Note areas of consensus and disagreement.]

---

## Prior Art

[If the learner is building something, what similar projects exist? What approaches have been tried? What worked and what didn't? Include specific project names, papers, and repos with links.]

---

## Curated Resources

[Organized by category. For each resource, include: title, link, one sentence on why it's useful for this specific project. Prioritize free/affordable, high-quality, and directly relevant.]

### [Category 1 — e.g., "Foundational Courses"]
- **[Title]** — [URL] — [Why it's relevant]

### [Category 2 — e.g., "Reference Implementations"]
- **[Title]** — [URL] — [Why it's relevant]

[Continue for each category...]

---

## Common Pitfalls

[Where do learners typically get stuck in this domain? What misconceptions are common? What do people wish they'd known earlier?]

---

## Recommendations for the Learning Plan

[Based on the research, what should the phase structure look like? What order makes sense? What should be front-loaded? What can be deferred? Are there any "must not skip" topics?]
```

---

## Guidelines

- Verify that linked resources actually exist — don't hallucinate URLs.
- Prioritize primary sources (official docs, original papers, author blogs) over aggregators.
- Keep the report scannable — someone should be able to read just the headers and bullet points and get the gist.
- The "Recommendations" section directly feeds into phase design — write it with that in mind.
- Length should match the domain's complexity. A report for "learn vim" might be 1 page. A report for "learn ML for production deployment" might be 10.
